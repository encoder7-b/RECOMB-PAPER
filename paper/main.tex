\begin{abstract}
\noindent Public immune-checkpoint-inhibitor (ICI) cohorts provide tumour transcriptomes for only 25--300 patients each. That is too few to estimate the gene--gene dependence that conditional inference requires, and response is often confounded with batch structure. Consequently, transcriptomic predictors of response rarely replicate. We present a framework that learns dependence structure (a sparse gene graph and latent-factor directions) once from large unlabelled external data, and eliminates all cohort-specific parameters by conditioning rather than estimation. The resulting gene-module test is exact in finite samples for any outcome distribution whenever the external structure contains the cohort's true structure. Evidence is combined across cohorts by a direction-consistent partial-conjunction procedure with false discovery rate (FDR) control. We show that misspecified structure induces an omitted-variable bias whose effect on FDR grows with sample size, and that over-spanning the external factor space removes it when the error lies in the factor span, though not when response-related dependence is missing from the graph. In simulations the test is calibrated in every setting and costs little power relative to an oracle, whereas marginal module tests and label-aware batch correction inflate FDR up to 2.8- and 4.5-fold. Under adversarial factor-span misspecification, over-spanning restores FDR to \advNewNoDropLo--\advNewNoDropHi. In a pre-registered, hash-sealed analysis of \nICI\ patients across ten ICI cohorts, no tested module carried detectable response information beyond the established T-effector/IFN-$\gamma$ signature, and discovery $p$-values were consistent with the global null. These results provide a leakage-free procedure for conditional biomarker discovery in small cohorts.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} conditional independence testing; false discovery rate control; immune checkpoint inhibitors; transcriptomic biomarkers; replicability; partial conjunction testing; external data integration; Gaussian graphical models; batch effects; pre-registration.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Code and data:} \repo\ (code, simulations, protocol, label hashes and fetch scripts for all public data).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Many studies have proposed transcriptomic predictors of response to immune checkpoint blockade. Few hold up in independent cohorts. Three features of the public data explain much of this.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Small cohorts.} Typical cohorts have 25--300 patients and 20{,}000 genes \citep{hugo2016,riaz2017,gide2019,liu2019,jung2019,mariathasan2018,mcdermott2018,braun2020}.
  \item \textbf{Response is entangled with batch:} drug, regimen, prior therapy, biopsy site and trial.
  \item \textbf{Leakage.} Cross-cohort correction that protects the outcome as a covariate inflates downstream significance \citep{nygaard2016batch}.
\end{itemize}

We ask a sharper question than ``is module $G$ associated with response?'' We ask whether $G$ carries information \emph{beyond all other measured genes}, including the established T-effector/IFN-$\gamma$ signature \citep{ayers2017ifng}, and whether that holds in more than one cohort.

\paragraph{Related work.} Table~\ref{tab:related} compares the closest methods. Model-X knockoffs and the conditional randomisation test (CRT) control error for conditional associations given the feature distribution \citep{candes2018panning}. The multi-environment knockoff filter adds cross-environment consistency \citep{li2022multienv}. Conditional knockoffs condition on sufficient statistics to avoid estimating parameters \citep{huang2020relaxing}, and summary-statistic knockoffs borrow dependence from reference panels \citep{he2022ghostknockoff}. A recent CRT variant uses external and unlabelled data to estimate the conditional distribution by transfer learning and a residual bootstrap \citep{crtstar2026}. Our method differs in what it takes from external data (\emph{structure only}: a graph support and a factor span). It also differs in how it handles everything else (exact elimination by conditioning, in closed form), and in its unit and target (gene modules, replicated across independent small cohorts with batch). Table~\ref{tab:related} compares assumptions and guarantees, not performance. The only empirical comparison with a knockoff-based multi-environment pipeline is in its oracle form (our first design, below), and we did not run CRT*.

\paragraph{A negative result motivated the design.} Our first design combined conditional knockoffs on an external graph with derandomised e-values \citep{ren2024derandomised} and partial conjunction. It made \emph{zero} discoveries in all 288 oracle settings we ran (knockoffs from the true model, $n$ up to 300, AUC up to 0.92), because knockoff+ needs more clean selections than 10--20 true signals can supply. Per-hypothesis tests do not have this floor.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item An exact conditional test for a gene set. External data supply only a graph support and a factor span, and every cohort-specific parameter is eliminated by conditioning (Proposition~\ref{prop:exact}, full proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}).
  \item A direction-consistent Stouffer partial-conjunction combiner, with multiplicity control across modules (BH, and BY under arbitrary dependence).
  \item An omitted-variable characterisation of misspecification: inflation grows like $\sqrt n$ and is driven by the residual correlation between the omitted direction and the outcome residual (Proposition~\ref{prop:bias}). Simulations confirm this. Over-spanning repairs factor-span errors, but it cannot repair omitted response-related graph edges, which inflate FDR up to \advNewDropHi.
  \item Evidence that label-aware batch correction and marginal module tests do not control FDR for this estimand.
  \item A pre-registered, hash-sealed analysis of \nICI\ patients, with a sealed replication audit, a real-data permutation null and exploratory checks.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Model and estimand}

In cohort $k$, row $i$ holds expression $X_i\in\mathbb R^p$, a response $Y_i$ and a batch label $b_i$ from a finite set. A marker set $B$ is never tested. For a gene set $G$ disjoint from $B$, the null is
\[
H_{G,k}:\ Y_i \indep X_{iG} \mid X_{i,-G},\, b_i .
\]

\begin{assumption}[Sampling]\label{as:iid}
Given the batch labels, the rows $(X_i,Y_i)$ are independent and identically distributed within each batch.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Factor-plus-graph Gaussian model]\label{as:gauss}
$X_i\mid b_i\sim N(\mu_{b_i},\Sigma)$ with $\Sigma=\Lambda\Lambda^\top+\Omega^{-1}$, where $\Lambda\in\mathbb R^{p\times k}$, $\Omega\succ0$, and $\Omega_{jl}\neq0$ only on the edges of a graph $\mathcal E$. Batch shifts only the mean.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Containment]\label{as:contain}
The external structure $(\hat{\mathcal E},\hat W)$ is independent of the cohort data and satisfies two conditions.
\begin{itemize}
  \item $N(G)\subseteq\hat N(G)$, where $N(G)$ is the set of genes outside $G$ that are adjacent to $G$ in $\mathcal E$.
  \item $\operatorname{colspan}(\Omega\Lambda)\subseteq\operatorname{colspan}(\hat W)$.
\end{itemize}
\end{assumption}

The design for $G$ is $D_G=[\,\mathbf 1,\ \text{batch dummies},\ X_{\hat N(G)},\ X_{-G}\hat W_{-G}\,]$, with rank $q$. The residual $r$ is any function of $(Y,X_{-G},b)$. We use $Y$ minus its least-squares fit on batch and the mean of $B$.

\subsection{The exact test}

Let $\tilde X_G$ and $\tilde r$ be the residuals of $X_G$ and $r$ after projection onto the columns of $D_G$. Define
\[
u=\tilde X_G^\top\tilde r/\|\tilde r\|, \qquad S=\tilde X_G^\top\tilde X_G-uu^\top .
\]
Wilks' statistic for adding $r$ to the regression of $X_G$ on $D_G$ is $\Lambda_W=1/(1+u^\top S^{-1}u)$.

\begin{proposition}[Exactness]\label{prop:exact}
Suppose Assumptions~\ref{as:iid}--\ref{as:contain} and $H_{G,k}$ hold, $g=|G|$, and $n-q-1\ge g$. Then, conditionally on $(X_{-G},Y,b)$, and therefore also unconditionally,
\[
F=\frac{n-q-g}{g}\,u^\top S^{-1}u\ \sim\ F_{g,\,n-q-g}.
\]
This holds for every conditional law of $Y$, and the law does not depend on $\mu$, $\Sigma$ or the batch effects. For $g=1$, or for any fixed projection $X_Ga$, it reduces to an exact $t$-test with $n-q-1$ degrees of freedom, whose sign is used.
\end{proposition}

The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}. Its three steps:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item By the Woodbury identity, $E[X_G\mid X_{-G},b]$ is linear in the neighbours $X_{N(G)}$ and in the $k$ combinations $X_{-G}(\Omega\Lambda)_{-G}$, with constant conditional covariance. Under containment this is $D_G\beta$.
  \item Under $H_{G,k}$ with independent rows, conditioning additionally on $Y$ leaves this law unchanged, so $D_G$ and $r$ can be treated as fixed.
  \item With a single added regressor, $u\sim N_g(0,\Sigma_c)$ is independent of $S\sim\mathrm{Wishart}_g(n-q-1,\Sigma_c)$. So $(n-q-1)u^\top S^{-1}u$ is Hotelling's $T^2$, which is exactly $F$-distributed for any $g$.
\end{enumerate}
Gene scales are fixed from external data, so the per-cohort design never uses cohort statistics of $X_G$.

\paragraph{Verification.} Our implementation agrees with independent implementations (statsmodels OLS for $g=1$, and multivariate OLS/MANOVA Wilks for $g=5$) to within \agreeMax\ in $|\log p|$. A numerical audit of Lemma~A1 over 200 random sparse-plus-factor models confirms that the true conditional-mean coefficients lie in the span of the true neighbours and $\Omega\Lambda$, and in any span containing $\Omega\Lambda$, to within $10^{-13}$. Dropping a single neighbour or factor direction breaks this. Under the conditional null, with a binary $Y$ that depends nonlinearly on genes outside $D_G$ and $20{,}000$ replicates per setting, rejection rates match nominal levels for $n$ from 15 to 100 and $g$ from 1 to 10 (Table~\ref{tab:exact}).

\subsection{External structure}

The external structure is learned from unlabelled expression of the matched tumour types (TCGA; \citealp{tcga2013pancan}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item standardise each gene;
  \item estimate $k$ principal factors $\hat\Lambda$;
  \item fit a neighbourhood-lasso graph to the factor residuals \citep{meinshausen2006}, take its union with each gene's top correlation partners, and cap the degree at 3;
  \item estimate $\hat\Omega$ on that graph.
\end{itemize}
Assumption~\ref{as:contain} needs containment, not recovery, so we inflate both parts. For the graph we take the union with correlation partners. For the factor span we use $\hat W=[\hat\Omega\hat\Lambda,\ \hat\Lambda]$ with over-specified $k=\mathrm{clip}(\lfloor n/25\rfloor,2,8)$ (\emph{over-spanning}). Each column costs one degree of freedom, and a module is skipped in a cohort if fewer than 8 remain.

\begin{remark}
Over-spanning makes containment of the factor span more likely, but it does not guarantee it. It cannot repair an omitted graph edge. Section~\ref{sec:sims} separates these cases.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Combining cohorts and modules}

\begin{lemma}[Partial conjunction]\label{lem:pc}
Suppose the cohorts are independent given the external structure, and let $H^{r/K}_G$ state that $G$ is non-null in fewer than $r$ of the $K$ cohorts in which $G$ is tested. For each sign $s\in\{\pm1\}$, let $p^{(s)}_{(1)}\le\dots\le p^{(s)}_{(K)}$ be the ordered exact one-sided $p$-values $1-\Phi(sz_k)$, and let $m=K-r+1$. Then
\[
P^{r/K}_G=\min\Bigl\{1,\,2\min_s\bar\Phi\Bigl(\textstyle\sum_{i\ge r}\Phi^{-1}\bigl(1-p^{(s)}_{(i)}\bigr)/\sqrt m\Bigr)\Bigr\}
\]
satisfies $\Pr(P^{r/K}_G\le t)\le t$ under $H^{r/K}_G$.
\end{lemma}

The idea is that at least $m$ cohorts are null and each $z_k$ is exactly $N(0,1)$ there. The $m$ largest $p$-values dominate a sorted set of $m$ independent uniforms, and the Stouffer combination is monotone (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}; \citealp{benjamini2008pc}).

Across modules we apply BH \citep{benjamini1995}. BH is guaranteed only when the null $p$-values are positively dependent (PRDS), which we cannot prove here, because modules share the residual $r$ and overlap in their designs. We therefore also report Benjamini--Yekutieli (BY), which is valid under arbitrary dependence. Cohort independence is a correctness requirement, which we enforce with a calibrated duplicate-patient screen (Section~\ref{sec:data}).

\paragraph{Untested cohorts.} A module may be untestable in a cohort because too few degrees of freedom remain. Testability depends only on expression, never on $Y$. So dropping that cohort from the module's combination (using the $K$ cohorts in which it is tested) keeps Lemma~\ref{lem:pc} valid by conditioning. The pre-registered rule instead set its $z$ to 0, a fixed $p=1/2$ that the lemma does not cover. We report both rules, and they agree (Section~\ref{sec:data}).

\subsection{Misspecification}

\begin{proposition}[Omitted-direction bias]\label{prop:bias}
Suppose $E[X_G\mid X_{-G},b]=D_G\beta+U\gamma$, where $U=X_{-G}c\notin\operatorname{colspan}(D_G)$. This happens when a true edge is missing from $\hat{\mathcal E}$ or when Assumption~\ref{as:contain} fails for $\hat W$. Then under $H_{G,k}$ the $t$-statistic for $r$ (with $g=1$) has non-centrality
\[
\delta_n=\sqrt n\,\gamma\,\frac{\mathrm{Cov}(r,U\mid D_G)}{\sigma_c\,\mathrm{sd}(r\mid D_G)}+o_p(\sqrt n).
\]
\end{proposition}

The bias is non-zero only when the omitted direction is correlated with the outcome \emph{residual} $r$. Being correlated with response is not enough. When it is correlated with $r$, the type-I error tends to 1 as $n$ grows. The derivation is in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\paragraph{Scope of the guarantee.} Proposition~\ref{prop:exact} is exact only under Assumptions~\ref{as:iid}--\ref{as:contain}, and outside them we claim no guarantee.
\begin{itemize}
  \item An omitted direction correlated with $r$ drives the type-I error to 1 (Proposition~\ref{prop:bias}). In adversarial simulations, missing response-related edges inflate FDR up to \advNewDropHi.
  \item Over-spanning repairs factor-span errors only.
  \item Patients shared between cohorts violate Lemma~\ref{lem:pc} (Section~\ref{sec:sims}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Pre-registration}

All label-free choices were frozen in a protocol and committed before any outcome was read:
\begin{itemize}
  \item gene universe (1500 genes) and marker set;
  \item modules (TCGA residual-correlation clusters);
  \item batch variables and response definitions;
  \item sealed cohorts, primary analysis ($r=1$, BH at 0.1) and replication rule.
\end{itemize}
The commit includes SHA-256 hashes of every label file, and the analysis refuses to read true labels unless the protocol is committed and every hash matches.

\section{Simulations}\label{sec:sims}

\paragraph{Design.} Each simulated dataset has:
\begin{itemize}
  \item 520 genes: 50 tested modules of 10, plus 20 marker genes whose mean drives response;
  \item AR(1) dependence within modules, and four latent factors $F_1,\dots,F_4$ ($F_1$ immune, $F_4$ diffuse);
  \item a two-level batch with gene-specific shifts and a response-rate shift;
  \item binary $Y$ with heterogeneous effects across cohorts.
\end{itemize}
Signal is \emph{sparse} (2 genes per module, random signs) or \emph{coherent} (all genes in a module, one sign). Effect sizes are set by the AUC of the signal genes' combined score, 0.65--0.80. External structure is learned from $N_{\rm ext}=500$ or $2000$ unlabelled samples. All results use $K=6$ cohorts and BH at $\alpha=0.1$, with $r=1$ unless stated.

\paragraph{Calibration.} Table~\ref{tab:null} reports 2000-replicate global-null runs with external structure learned from $N_{\rm ext}=500$, the size of a TCGA tumour type.
\begin{itemize}
  \item FDR, which under the global null equals the chance of any false discovery, stays within Monte Carlo error of $\alpha=0.1$ or below at every $n$.
  \item Among feasible tests the per-test rejection rate matches 0.05.
  \item At $n=25$ only about half of the modules are feasible. That is a loss of power, not of validity.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Factor-span misspecification.} With the graph held at the truth, we varied the factor span directly (Table~\ref{tab:factor}). For each omitted factor we also measured $|\rho|$, the correlation between the omitted direction and $r$ given $D_G$ (the driver in Proposition~\ref{prop:bias}). For two independent vectors of length 300 the noise level is about 0.046.
\begin{itemize}
  \item FDR stayed at or below nominal for the true span and for a span containing it.
  \item It also stayed nominal when we omitted $F_1$ (immune) or $F_2$, whose $|\rho|$ was at or below the noise level. $F_1$ is strongly linked to response, but that link is absorbed by the marker adjustment in $r$.
  \item Omitting the diffuse factor $F_4$ under coherent signal gave $|\rho|$ of 2.5 times the noise level. FDR rose with $n$, to 0.150 at $n=300$.
\end{itemize}
Correlation with response is therefore not what matters. Correlation with the residual is, as Proposition~\ref{prop:bias} predicts.

\paragraph{Graph misspecification under adversarial edges.} We added 40 true edges joining signal genes to genes in null modules (partial correlation 0.25), then deleted a fraction of them from the learned graph (Fig.~\ref{fig:adversarial}).
\begin{itemize}
  \item With the default span ($k=4$, $\hat W=\hat\Omega\hat\Lambda$), FDR reached \advOldNoDropHi\ at $n=300$ even with nothing deleted, because $\hat W$ was estimated with error. With oracle structure it was 0.10.
  \item Over-spanning restored FDR to \advNewNoDropLo--\advNewNoDropHi\ with nothing deleted. Power was unchanged at $n=300$ and lower at $n=100$ (0.12 against 0.20).
  \item Deleting true response-related edges still inflates FDR, up to \advNewDropHi. Over-spanning cannot repair that.
  \item Random missing edges, which rarely touch response-relevant genes, left calibration unchanged.
  \item Distilling the outcome residual with a lasso \citep{liu2022dcrt} did not help.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Dependent cohorts (shared patients).} Lemma~\ref{lem:pc} assumes independent cohorts. To see what happens when that fails, we made each pair of adjacent cohorts share a fraction of patients (identical $X$ and $Y$, no batch mismatch, oracle structure, 500 replicates; Table~\ref{tab:shared}).
\begin{itemize}
  \item Sharing 10\% of patients roughly doubled FDR under the global null at $r=1$.
  \item The overlap correlates adjacent $z$-scores only modestly, but BH's first rejection lies far in the tail, where a slightly wider null distribution matters a great deal.
  \item Replication at $r=2$ is more robust.
\end{itemize}
This is why the duplicate screen is part of the method.

\section{Real data}\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Cohorts.} The cohorts are listed in Table~\ref{tab:cohorts}.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Sources:} GEO (Hugo, Riaz, Jung, GSE176307; \citealp{gse176307}); iAtlas-harmonised cBioPortal studies (Gide, Liu, IMvigor210, IMmotion150; \citealp{iatlas,cerami2012cbioportal}); and the Braun supplementary tables.
  \item \textbf{Labels:} response is CR/PR against SD/PD. Jung has no RECIST data in GEO, so we used durable benefit (PFS $\ge$180 days). Samples are pre-treatment, one per patient.
  \item \textbf{Modules:} the fixed recipe produced \nModules\ modules covering about 400 of the 1500 genes.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Duplicate screen.} Each gene is ranked within its cohort, five principal components are removed, and samples are correlated across cohorts. We calibrated this on a positive control: Hugo from GEO against the iAtlas re-processing, the same 26 patients through different pipelines. True pairs scored $\rho\ge0.67$, non-pairs $\le0.27$, and impossible NSCLC--ccRCC pairs $\le0.28$. Ten pairs crossed the threshold of 0.5. None was plausible as a shared patient, and all 14 samples involved were excluded.

\paragraph{Design.} Eight cohorts (\nDisc\ patients) were used for discovery. IMvigor210 and Braun nivolumab (\nSeal\ patients) were sealed for replication. The sunitinib and everolimus arms (\nCtrl\ patients) were descriptive controls.

\paragraph{Pre-registered result.} There were \nDiscovered\ discoveries at $r=1$ and $r=2$, with BH, BY and Simes. The marginal and pooled baselines also made none. Dropping untested cohorts instead of setting $z=0$ (\nUntested\ of \nCells\ module--cohort cells were untested) also gave 0 discoveries, with minimum $P=\sensMinP$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item The strongest module, the metallothioneins MT1G/A/H/M/E, had $P=\bestP$. A single BH rejection would need $P\le\bhThresh$. Its evidence was positive in 7 of 8 discovery cohorts but negative in both sealed cohorts.
  \item Discovery $p$-values were consistent with uniform: \nBelow\ of \nModules\ fell below 0.05, and the Kolmogorov--Smirnov test against uniform gave $p=\ksP$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:real}a).
  \item Sealed-cohort ``replication'' occurred for \nReplicated\ modules, against \expReplicated\ expected by chance.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Real-data permutation null.} We permuted response within the batch strata of every discovery cohort \permN\ times. This keeps the real expression covariance and the real external structure.
\begin{itemize}
  \item The per-test rejection rate at 0.05 was \permRate.
  \item The chance of any discovery was \permBH\ with BH and \permBY\ with BY at $r=1$, and \permBHtwo\ with BH at $r=2$.
  \item The real minimum $p$-value sat at the \permMinQ\ quantile of its permutation distribution.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Exploratory checks (post-unsealing, logged as deviations).}
\begin{itemize}

  \item \textbf{Incremental prediction} (Table~\ref{tab:explore}). Models trained on the discovery cohorts and scored on held-out cohorts gained nothing over the T-effector score. That held when adding any single module ($\Delta$AUC $\le$ \modDeltaMax) and when adding an elastic net over all 1500 genes (\enetGenes\ non-zero genes).

\section{Discussion}

The method separates what small cohorts can supply from what they cannot. External data supply the dependence structure. Each cohort's nuisance parameters are eliminated exactly by conditioning. Validity then rests on containment, and the simulations show which violations of containment matter: those whose omitted direction is correlated with the outcome residual. Over-spanning repairs errors that lie in the factor span. It cannot repair omitted response-related edges, which inflated FDR up to \advNewDropHi\ in our adversarial simulations. Shared patients between cohorts also break the guarantee, which is why the duplicate screen is part of the method rather than a preprocessing detail.

The real-data result is a null for the tested modules. It says that the analysis found no significant incremental conditional association among \nModules\ TCGA-derived modules beyond the T-effector score. It does not show that no other gene set carries such information.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Exactness assumes conditional Gaussianity on the log scale.
  \item The modules cover a minority of genes.
  \item Power is low below about 100 patients per cohort.
  \item BH is not guaranteed under the dependence between modules (BY is).
  \item The Braun expression matrix was used as released by the authors.
  \item The estimand is conditional association, not causation or clinical utility.
\end{itemize}

\section{Proofs}\label{app:proofs}

\paragraph{Lemma A1 (conditional mean).} Write $W=\Omega\Lambda$ and $M=(I_k+\Lambda^\top\Omega\Lambda)^{-1}$. By the Woodbury identity, the precision is
\[
\Theta=\Sigma^{-1}=\Omega-WMW^\top .
\]
For a fixed batch $b$, Gaussian conditioning gives
\[
E[X_G\mid X_{-G}=x,b]=\mu_{bG}-\Theta_{GG}^{-1}\Theta_{G,-G}(x-\mu_{b,-G}),\qquad \operatorname{Var}(X_G\mid X_{-G},b)=\Theta_{GG}^{-1}=:\Sigma_c .
\]
Now $\Theta_{G,-G}=\Omega_{G,-G}-W_GMW_{-G}^\top$, and the columns of $\Omega_{G,-G}$ are zero outside $N(G)$. So
\[
-\Theta_{GG}^{-1}\Theta_{G,-G}x=x_{N(G)}A_1+(x^\top W_{-G})A_2
\]
for some matrices $A_1$ and $A_2$. The terms in $\mu$ are linear in the batch dummies. Under Assumption~\ref{as:contain}, $W=\hat WC$ for some matrix $C$. Deleting rows preserves this, so $W_{-G}=\hat W_{-G}C$, and $X_{N(G)}$ is a subset of the columns of $X_{\hat N(G)}$. Hence $E[X_G\mid X_{-G},b]=D_G\beta$, with constant covariance $\Sigma_c$ that does not depend on $b$.

\paragraph{Lemma A2 (conditioning on $Y$).} By Assumption~\ref{as:iid} the rows are independent given $b$. Under $H_{G,k}$, $Y_i\indep X_{iG}\mid(X_{i,-G},b_i)$. Therefore the joint conditional law of the rows $X_{iG}$ given $(X_{-G},Y,b)$ is the product of the laws given $(X_{i,-G},b_i)$, that is,
\[
X_G=D_G\beta+\mathcal E,
\]
where $\mathcal E$ has i.i.d.\ $N(0,\Sigma_c)$ rows independent of $(X_{-G},Y,b)$. Both $D_G$ and $r$ are functions of $(X_{-G},Y,b)$, so they are fixed under this conditioning. The residual $r$ may depend on $Y$ in any way.

\paragraph{Lemma A3 (exact law for one added regressor).} Condition on $(X_{-G},Y,b)$ and assume $\tilde r\neq0$. Let $P$ be the projection onto $\operatorname{colspan}(D_G)$, let $v=\tilde r/\|\tilde r\|$ (a unit vector orthogonal to $\operatorname{colspan}(D_G)$), and let $Q=I-P-vv^\top$, a projection of rank $\nu=n-q-1$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Since $(I-P)D_G=0$, we have $u=\tilde X_G^\top v=\mathcal E^\top v\sim N_g(0,\Sigma_c)$.
  \item Similarly $S=\mathcal E^\top Q\mathcal E\sim \mathrm{Wishart}_g(\nu,\Sigma_c)$.
  \item $u$ and $S$ are independent, because $Qv=0$ and the rows of $\mathcal E$ are Gaussian.
\end{itemize}
Hence $T^2=\nu\,u^\top S^{-1}u$ is Hotelling's $T^2_{g,\nu}$, and $\frac{\nu-g+1}{g\nu}T^2\sim F_{g,\nu-g+1}$ whenever $\nu\ge g$. Since $\nu-g+1=n-q-g$, this proves Proposition~\ref{prop:exact}.

Wilks' $\Lambda_W=\det S/\det(S+uu^\top)=1/(1+u^\top S^{-1}u)$. The conditional law does not depend on the conditioning values, so it also holds unconditionally. Replacing $X_G$ by a fixed projection $X_Ga$ gives the $g=1$ case, $t=\sqrt\nu\,u/\sqrt S\sim t_\nu$, with the sign carried by $u$. The law involves neither $\beta$ nor $\Sigma_c$, which is the sense in which the nuisance parameters are eliminated.

\paragraph{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:pc}.} Fix $s$. Under $H^{r/K}_G$ at least $m$ cohorts are null. In those, $z_k\sim N(0,1)$ exactly (by Proposition~\ref{prop:exact}, applied with $\Phi^{-1}\circ F_t$), so their one-sided $p$-values are independent and uniform, given the external structure. By pigeonhole, the $j$-th largest of all $K$ $p$-values is at least the $j$-th largest of the null ones, for $j=1,\dots,m$. The $m$ largest of $m_0\ge m$ independent uniforms are stochastically no smaller than $m$ sorted independent uniforms. The Stouffer $p$-value is non-decreasing in each argument, so it is super-uniform. The factor 2 is a Bonferroni correction over $s$ \citep{benjamini2008pc}. If $G$ is untested in some cohorts, condition on the set of tested cohorts. It is a function of $X$ only, so under the null the $z_k$ of the tested null cohorts remain independent $N(0,1)$, and the argument applies with $K$ replaced by the number of tested cohorts.

\paragraph{Proof sketch of Proposition~\ref{prop:bias}.} Write $X_G=D_G\beta+U\gamma+\mathcal E$ with $g=1$. Then
\[
u=\tilde r^\top(\tilde U\gamma+\tilde{\mathcal E})/\|\tilde r\| .
\]
The term $\tilde r^\top\tilde{\mathcal E}/\|\tilde r\|\sim N(0,\sigma_c^2)$ as before. The term $\tilde r^\top\tilde U\gamma/\|\tilde r\|=\sqrt n\,\gamma\,\widehat{\mathrm{Cov}}(r,U\mid D)/\widehat{\mathrm{sd}}(r\mid D)$, and the sample moments converge. Meanwhile $S/\nu\to\sigma_c^2+\gamma^2\mathrm{Var}(U\mid D,r)$, which is bounded. Dividing gives the stated non-centrality. It is non-zero only if $U$ is correlated with $r$ given $D_G$.
